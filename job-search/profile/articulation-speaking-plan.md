# Articulation & Speaking Skills - Practice Plan

> Built after the 7-Eleven final round came down to articulation. Good news: the fix is one of the most trainable skills there is, and the techniques the coaches agree on are exactly the ones from your mock debriefs, slow down, cut fillers, structure before you speak, land the point and stop. This is a 4-week routine, ~15 to 20 minutes a day, with drills, frameworks, and a way to measure progress. Golden rule: **structure first, then fill. Rambling happens when you start talking before you know your last sentence.**

---

## PART 0 - Your specific tells (what we're actually fixing)
From the mocks, your pattern is not "bad speaking", it's four fixable habits:
1. **Trailing, unfinished sentences** to answers fade out instead of landing ("...year on year price movements, quarter-"). **#1 fix.**
2. **Rambling before the point** to you circle the topic before saying the thing.
3. **Filler words** to "uh", "like", "you know" when you're thinking.
4. **Pace too fast under nerves** to words slur together and clarity drops.

The target: **clear, concise, confident.** Say the point, support it, land the last line, stop.

---

## PART 1 - Three frameworks that kill rambling (learn these cold)

**PREP** (for any opinion or answer) to **P**oint, **R**eason, **E**xample, **P**oint.
- State your position, say why, give one concrete example, restate the position. Instant structure your listener can follow. Toastmasters calls it the easiest one to learn.
- *Example: "I'd prioritise the urgent one first (Point). It unblocks the client and it's short (Reason). Last quarter I did exactly that with a tender eval before a big build (Example). So: quick-and-urgent first, protect the big one (Point)."*

**What / So what / Now what** (for reacting to information, a chart, a question, an email).
- What do I see, why does it matter, what do I do next. Three sentences. Drill it after reading anything and it becomes automatic in the moment.

**BLUF** to **B**ottom **L**ine **U**p **F**ront.
- Lead with the answer, then explain. "The short answer is yes, and here's why." Stops you burying the point.

**STAR** (for stories, you already have this) to Situation, Task, Action, Result. Always end on the Result with a number.

**The meta-rule:** before you open your mouth, know your **last sentence**. If you know where you're landing, you stop circling.

---

## PART 2 - The core mechanics (the how)
1. **Slow down.** Rushing is what slurs words and invites fillers. Deliberately drop your pace, pause at commas and full stops. Slower reads as *more* confident, not less.
2. **Replace every filler with a silent pause.** A pause feels like an eternity to you but signals confidence and control to the listener. Silence > "um".
3. **Breathe before you answer.** One diaphragm breath before speaking cuts the nervous-energy that fuels fillers. Buy the second: "Good question, let me think about that."
4. **One idea per sentence.** Short sentences. Full stop. Next idea. Long run-ons are where you lose the thread and trail off.
5. **Land the last line and stop.** Say the result, then close your mouth. Do not add "...so yeah" or trail into "...and stuff like that."
6. **Lead with the point (BLUF)**, then support it.

---

## PART 3 - The daily drills (~15 to 20 min)
Pick 3 to 4 each day. All out loud. Record when noted.

- **Tongue twisters (2 min)** to warm up enunciation. "Red leather yellow leather", "unique New York", any you like. Slow, crisp, then speed up.
- **Read aloud, slowly (3 min)** to read a paragraph from an article at a deliberately slow pace, clear and expressive, pausing at punctuation. Trains pace + clarity.
- **60-second Table Topic (core drill)** to pick a random topic (a word, an object, an opinion), set a 60s timer, and speak using **PREP**, aiming for **zero fillers** and a clean final line. Start easy ("my favourite meal"), progress to abstract ("is remote work good for teams").
- **Filler tally (record, 2 min)** to record a 2-minute impromptu answer, play it back, and count every "um/like/you know". Write the number down. Watch it fall week over week.
- **The pause drill** to redo the same 2-minute answer, forcing a **silent pause** everywhere you'd normally say a filler. Feels weird, works fast.
- **What / So what / Now what** to after any article or email, say a 3-sentence out-loud summary. Trains structure-on-demand.
- **One-breath answer** to answer a question in a single, complete, landed sentence. Forces concision.

---

## PART 4 - The 4-week progression

**Week 1 - Awareness + mechanics.** Record a 2-min baseline (keep it, you'll compare). Daily: tongue twisters + slow read-aloud + one filler tally. Goal: notice your fillers and slow your pace.

**Week 2 - Structure.** Daily: 3x 60-second Table Topics using **PREP** and **BLUF**, each with a clean landing line. Add the What/So-what/Now-what drill on one article a day. Goal: never ramble, always land.

**Week 3 - Pressure + stories.** Take your interview **STAR stories** and deliver each in **90 seconds, timed**, ending on the number. Do harder impromptu topics. Have me (or a friend) throw questions and raise a hand on every filler. Goal: structure holds up under pressure.

**Week 4 - Integration.** Full mock interviews and real conversations. Record a fresh 2-min answer and compare to your Week-1 baseline. Apply the habits in actual meetings. Goal: it's becoming automatic.

---

## PART 5 - Measure it (so you know it's working)
Track weekly:
- **Fillers per minute** (from the recording tally) to target: trending down.
- **Answer length** to are you landing within 60 to 90 seconds, or overrunning.
- **Clean landing?** to did you end on a full, deliberate sentence, yes or no.
- **Pace** to could you hear every word, or did it blur.

Keep the numbers in a note. Progress you can see keeps you going, and the Week-1-vs-Week-4 recording will genuinely surprise you.

---

## PART 6 - Real-world reps (beyond drills, this is where it sticks)
- **Toastmasters.** Their "Table Topics" segment is literally impromptu-speaking practice with live feedback. One club near you or online is the single highest-leverage thing you can add. Worth it.
- **Voice notes.** Answer a question as a voice memo to yourself, then listen back. Cheap, private, brutally effective.
- **Teach a concept simply.** Explain something technical (elasticity, a p-value) to an imaginary non-expert in plain words. Builds clarity and the "translate for the audience" muscle.
- **In real meetings.** Lead with the point, use PREP, and pause instead of "um". Every meeting is a rep.
- **Use me.** Run mock Q&A on a schedule, I'll tally fillers, time your answers, and flag every trailing ending. I'm a free, always-on Table Topics partner.

---

## PART 7 - Tools
- **Your phone's voice recorder** to the only essential tool. Record, listen, tally, repeat.
- **AI speech coaches (optional):** apps like **Yoodli** or **Poised** give automated feedback on filler words, pace, and clarity from your recordings. Handy for objective numbers.
- **A timer** to for the 60 and 90 second drills.

---

## The one habit that changes everything
Before you answer anything, take a breath and **know your last sentence.** Structure with PREP, keep sentences short, replace fillers with a pause, and land the point. You already have the substance, this is just training the delivery to match it. Four weeks of 15-minute reps and the next close call goes your way.
